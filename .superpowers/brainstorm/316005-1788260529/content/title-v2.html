The title settle you spotted

You were right, and it is not a layout bug — measured x is 0.000 at every frame in both versions.

What you saw. While a transform is applied the browser composites the text on its own layer and antialiases it in grayscale. The moment the transform ends it drops back to normal subpixel (RGB) antialiasing. The glyph edges gain and lose colour fringing, and the eye reads that as a small sideways nudge that "firms up" at the end. Nothing moved. The pixels changed.

This is the same effect the spec already cites when it rejected the landing-page parallax: "text translated at fractional pixels re-rasterises and reads soft."

Fire them a few times each, and watch the moment the new title arrives. In A it settles. In B it does not, because no transform is ever applied — so the text is rasterised the same way for the whole journey and at rest.

Why animating layout is fine here

The usual objection is that top costs a layout pass per frame while transform is compositor-only. That objection is about animating big subtrees. This is two absolutely-positioned spans inside a 36px box — and we already measured that animating the entire sidebar's width, with six columns and 72 cards reflowing behind it, holds a flat 16.7ms/frame. Two spans are far below that. Correct rendering is worth more than a compositor optimisation nobody can perceive.

B — layout-driven (recommended)

Animate top inside the existing mask. No layer promotion at any point, so the antialiasing is identical mid-flight and at rest, and there is no settle. Applies to the board title and to any other masked text move this phase introduces.

Drop the slide entirely

Every title change — switch and rename alike — becomes a one-frame swap. No settle because there is no animation. Cost: the header stops being part of the same gesture as the columns during a board switch, which was the reason the spec chose the slide over a swap.
